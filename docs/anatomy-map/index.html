Why the mean sits closer
to the lower pressure.

Mean arterial pressure (MAP, or MAD in this repo) gives diastolic pressure twice the weight of systolic pressure. This measuring bench makes the weighting visible.

MAP ≈ D + ⅓ × (S − D)
80 + (120 − 80) / 3 = 93.3 mmHg

Pulse pressure: 40 mmHg
One third adds 13.3 mmHg

Try moving each end by 30.

From 120/80, raising systolic pressure by 30 raises MAP by 10. Raising diastolic pressure by 30 raises MAP by 20. The output sits closer to the diastolic end, so it follows that end more strongly.

Why that weighting?

MAP is the time average over a heartbeat. At usual heart rates, more time is spent in diastole. This formula approximates the waveform; heart rate, rhythm and pulse shape can change its accuracy.
